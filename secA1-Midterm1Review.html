Why or why not?

    Let \(\v{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) \(\v{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) and \(\v{v}_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Is this set \(\set{\v{v}_1, \v{v}_2, \v{v}_3}\) an orthogonal set? Why or why not?
    🔗
    Answer.
    Nope
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Let \(\v{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) \(\v{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) and \(\v{v}_3 = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ -1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Is this set \(\set{\v{v}_1, \v{v}_2, \v{v}_3}\) an orthogonal set? Why or why not?
    🔗
    Answer.
    Yep
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Let \(\v{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) \(\v{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -6 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}\text{,}\) and \(\v{v}_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ 2 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Is this set \(\set{\v{v}_1, \v{v}_2, \v{v}_3}\) an orthogonal set? Why or why not?
    🔗
    Is this set \(\set{\v{v}_1, \v{v}_2, \v{v}_3}\) an orthonormal set? If not, normalize these vectors to create an orthonormal set?
    🔗
    Answer.
    Yep to the orthogonal set and nope to the orthonormal set.
    🔗
    The normalized version is \(\left\{\begin{bmatrix} \frac{2}{7} \\ \frac{3}{7} \\ \frac{6}{7} \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} \frac{3}{7} \\ -\frac{6}{7} \\ \frac{2}{7} \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} \frac{6}{7} \\ \frac{2}{7} \\ -\frac{3}{7} \end{bmatrix}\right\}\)
    🔗

    1. Find the dimension of \(\Col A\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find the dimension of \(\Nul A\text{.}\)
      🔗

    1. Find the dimension of \(\Col B\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find the dimension of \(\Nul B\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Let \(A\) be an \(n \times n\) invertible matrix.
    1. What is the dimension of \(\Col A\text{?}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. What is the dimension of \(\Nul A\text{?}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Answer.
    \(\dim \Col A = n\) and \(\dim \Nul A = 0\)
    🔗
    P.S: the matrix in the previous exercise is invertible, so you see that every column has a pivot position (and hence \(\dim \Col A = n\)). By the Rank Nullity Theorem, \(\dim \Nul A = 0\text{.}\)

    Worksheet Eigen-related Stuff
    

    1.

    Let \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \amp -1 \\ 1 \amp 3 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Find all the eigenvalues of this matrix, and determine the corresponding eigenspace.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The eigenvalue is \(\lambda = 4\text{,}\) with the multiplicity of \(2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Its corresponding eigenspace is \(E_4 = \span \left\{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\right\}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Let \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \amp -2 \amp -1 \\ -1 \amp 1 \amp -1 \\ -1 \amp -2 \amp 2 \end{bmatrix}\text{.}\) Find all the eigenvalues of this matrix.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The eigenvalues are \(\lambda = 3\) (with the multiplicity of \(2\)) and \(\lambda = -1\) (with the multiplicity of \(1\))
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Diagonalize the matrix \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \amp -5 \\ -3 \amp 2 \end{bmatrix}\) if possible. If not, state why.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Yep, \(A\) is diagonalizable.
    🔗
    \(D = \begin{bmatrix} 7 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp -1 \end{bmatrix}\) and \(P = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \amp 1 \\ -3 \amp 1 \end{bmatrix}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Diagonalize the matrix \(B = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \amp 2 \amp 2 \\ -2 \amp 3 \amp 1 \\ -2 \amp 2 \amp 4 \end{bmatrix}\) if possible. If not, state why.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Yep \(B\) is diagonalizable.
    🔗
    \(D = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 2 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 0 \amp 3 \end{bmatrix}\) and \(P = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \amp 0 \amp 1 \\ 1 \amp -1 \amp 0 \\ 0 \amp 1 \amp 2 \end{bmatrix}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    Diagonalize the matrix \(C = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \amp -6 \amp -4 \\ 5 \amp -11 \amp -6 \\ -6 \amp 9 \amp 4 \end{bmatrix}\) if possible. If not, state why.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Nope \(C\) is not diagonalizable.
